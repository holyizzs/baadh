Case Study: Kedarnath 2013 Cloudburst & GLOF

Mandakini River Valley, Rudraprayag, Uttarakhand • 16–17 June 2013

1. The Meteorological Anatomy

On 15–16 June 2013, an interaction between a strong Western Disturbance and early monsoon easterlies produced extreme precipitation over the Kedarnath catchment. The Chorabari Glacier received over 375 mm of rain in 24 hours (570 mm over 48 hours). This torrential downpour saturated the glacial moraine and triggered debris flows into Chorabari Lake (Gandhi Sarovar).

2. Moraine Dam Breach

At 06:45 AM on June 17, the moraine dam holding Chorabari Lake ruptured, unleashing 400,000 m³ of impounded water and thousands of tons of massive granite boulders directly onto the Kedarnath shrine settlement within minutes, washing away the entire downstream town of Rambara.

3. How RAINFO Solves This

By fusing 6-hour rainfall telemetry (120 mm at 12:00 PM June 16) with 82% antecedent soil saturation and steep 38° catchment slope, the RAINFO predictor engine generates a HIGH WARNING at 12:00 PM (18 hours prior) and escalates to a CRITICAL EMERGENCY RED ALERT at 03:00 PM, providing over 7.5 hours of calibrated advance warning before peak flood inundation.

Designated High-Ground Evacuation Site:
🏛 GMVN High Complex, Kedarnath Ridge (Elevation: 3,650m) — Located 0.8 km north of the valley floor, completely outside the debris surge path.

Case Study: Chamoli 2021 Rock/Ice Avalanche Flood

Rishiganga & Dhauliganga Valleys, Chamoli, Uttarakhand • 7 February 2021

1. The Geophysical Mechanism

At ~10:20 AM on 7 February 2021, an unstable wedge of approximately 27 million m³ of rock and glacier ice detached from the steep crest of Ronti Peak (~5,600m) in the Nanda Devi sanctuary. The mass dropped 1,800 meters onto the valley floor, pulverizing into a hyper-concentrated slurry.

2. Downstream Surge Propagation

The debris avalanche melted glacial ice through intense friction, instantly transforming into an enormous flash flood of mud, rocks, and water traveling at 25–30 m/s (~100 km/h). It reached the Rishiganga Hydropower Project (Raini village) in 10 minutes and the Tapovan Vishnugad Project (25 km away) in 50 minutes, trapping over 200 workers in intake tunnels.

3. How RAINFO Solves This

Unlike standard rain gauges, RAINFO incorporates upstream ultrasonic river-stage gauge nodes and acoustic vibration sensors. Within 90 seconds of the Ronti collapse, the sudden 18-meter river swell triggers an automated LoRa mesh broadcast. This delivers 35 to 45 minutes of acoustic siren warning at the Tapovan dam site, providing sufficient lead time for tunnel worker evacuation.

Designated High-Ground Evacuation Site:
🏛 Police Lines High Ground Camp, Gopeshwar (Elevation: 1,550m) & Upper Ridge Pavilion at Tapovan (1,920m).
